## 2. Growth: WHAT → WHY → HOW → RECOGNIZE → WHAT NEXT

> **GROWTH DECISION CHAIN**
> - WHAT: growth is a trajectory, not a single percentile. WHY: nutrition affects weight first; endocrine and skeletal disorders may primarily affect height.
> - HOW: verify technique/age, select chart, plot serial measurements, calculate height velocity and genetic target, assess body proportions and puberty.
> - RECOGNIZE: crossing ≥2 major centile lines, height <−2 SDS, poor velocity, disproportion, unexplained edema/weight loss, height far from mid-parental target.
> - WHAT NEXT: nutrition/feeding assessment for weight-first faltering; targeted screening for systemic disease; bone age and endocrine referral if height-first pattern; genetics/skeletal evaluation for dysmorphism or disproportion.
> Source: Day 13 > Growth Charts / IAP Growth Charts 2026

| Age / setting | Reference | Decisive exam distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Birth to 5 y | WHO 2006 growth standards | Z scores for WAZ, HAZ, WHZ, BMI-for-age; WHO is prescriptive. |
| 5–18 y India | IAP 2015 Indian reference | BMI adult-equivalent 23 and 27 lines for overweight/obesity. |
| Preterm infant | Corrected-age-appropriate preterm chart per follow-up programme | Do not plot chronological age without correcting when required; IAP 2026 cites INTERGROWTH-21st. |
| Known syndrome | Syndrome-specific charts plus population charts | Syndrome-specific trend does not replace general nutritional evaluation. |

| Growth indicator | What it detects | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| WAZ | Underweight | Acute vs chronic mechanism alone |
| HAZ / LAZ | Stunting, linear-growth impairment | Specific endocrine diagnosis |
| WHZ / WLZ | Wasting / acute malnutrition | Long-term linear deficit |
| MUAC 6–59 mo | Acute malnutrition / mortality risk | Not the same as height-for-age |
| BMI-for-age | Thinness or excess adiposity | Cannot substitute for body composition |

> **MUST-KNOW GROWTH NUMBERS**
> - Short stature: height <−2 SDS (about 3rd centile); severe <−3 SDS merits prompt evaluation.
> - IAP 2026 rough linear growth velocity: 0–1 y ≈25 cm/y; 1–2 y ≈12 cm/y; 2–4 y ≈7–8 cm/y; 4 y–puberty ≈5–6 cm/y.
> - Mid-parental height: boy (father + mother + 13 cm)/2; girl (father + mother −13 cm)/2; IAP 2026 target band about ±6 cm.
> - Height velocity (cm/year) = (height2 − height1) ×12 / elapsed months; use standardized serial measurements, usually ≥6 months apart.
> Source: Day 13 > Must-Know Numbers; IAP 2026 Growth Charts

## 3. Short stature and growth faltering — discriminate before tests

| Pattern | Favors | Decisive clue / first work-up |
|---|---|---|
| Low weight precedes reduced height | Inadequate intake, malabsorption, chronic inflammation | Detailed feeding, stool/illness history, serial weights; investigate selectively. |
| Height slows while weight preserved/increased | Endocrine cause | Bone age; TSH/FT4, IGF-1 axis as clinically indicated; refer. |
| Normal velocity + short parents | Familial short stature | Bone age often ≈ chronological age; within genetic target. |
| Late puberty + delayed bone age | Constitutional delay | Family history of late maturation; normal/near-normal interval velocity. |
| Disproportion or skeletal deformity | Skeletal dysplasia / rickets | Upper/lower ratio, arm span, targeted skeletal evaluation. |
| Short girl + Turner clues | Turner syndrome | Karyotype even if phenotype is subtle. |
| Persistent SGA without catch-up | SGA-related short stature | Review age, growth trend, systemic causes; endocrine referral after expected catch-up period. |

> **ALGORITHM 1 — Short stature**
> 1. Confirm accurate stadiometry and age/sex chart; calculate height SDS.
> 2. Review ≥6–12-month growth velocity and parental target height.
> 3. Classify weight-first, height-first, proportionate or disproportionate pattern.
> 4. Look for red flags: puberty abnormality, dysmorphism, chronic disease, neurologic or skeletal features.
> 5. Order targeted CBC, ESR/CRP, thyroid tests, renal/liver tests, celiac serology + total IgA, bone age or genetic tests when indicated.
> 6. Treat the cause, refer to endocrine/genetics when indicated, and repeat standardized measures.
> Source: Day 13 > Short Stature / IAP 2026

> **ALGORITHM 2 — Growth faltering / poor weight gain**
> 1. Validate weight/length, growth chart and prior trajectory.
> 2. Take detailed milk transfer, food frequency, texture, feeding interaction, illness and psychosocial history.
> 3. Assess hydration, edema, micronutrient signs, swallowing/aspiration and chronic disease red flags.
> 4. Determine whether acute malnutrition criteria or serious illness require urgent referral.
> 5. Implement nutrition/lactation plan and targeted testing; monitor serial weight gain and developmental progress.
> Source: Day 13 > Growth Faltering; Day 14 > Feeding

## 4. Developmental milestones, delay and regression

| Age | Gross motor | Fine motor / social / language (approximate IAP 2026 anchors) |
|---|---|---|
| 6 wk–2 mo | Lifts chin briefly | Social smile ~6 wk; early vocalization |
| 3–4 mo | Head control developing | Reaches, coos, tracks |
| 6 mo | Rolls; sits with support | Transfers hand-to-hand; monosyllabic babble |
| 9 mo | Sits unsupported; pulls to stand | Immature pincer; gestures/stranger anxiety; bisyllabic babble |
| 12–15 mo | Cruises / walks with support then independent | Mature pincer; first meaningful words; points/shows |
| 18 mo | Walks confidently, stairs with hand held | Spoon use; imitation; meaningful words; check for regression |
| 2 y | Runs, stairs two-feet-per-step | Two-word combinations; 6-block tower (teaching anchor) |
| 3 y | Tricycle/jumps | Copies circle; pretend play; conversational speech |
| 4–5 y | Hops at 4; skips at 5 | Copies cross ~4; triangle ~5; cooperative play |

> **DEVELOPMENTAL RED FLAGS / NEXT ACTION**
> - Any loss of previously acquired words, social skills or motor skills = regression → urgent assessment; do not reassure based on prior normal milestones.
> - Not sitting unsupported around 9 months, not walking independently by 18 months, absent meaningful words/joint attention or no pretend play at expected age warrants formal assessment.
> - Isolated expressive language delay requires hearing evaluation; receptive/social-communication deficits suggest broader neurodevelopmental concern.
> - Global developmental delay (GDD): significant delay in at least two developmental domains, generally in children <5 years; diagnosis requires standardized assessment.
> - Preterm correction: corrected age = chronological age − (40 − GA at birth); distinguish PMA = GA at birth + chronological age.
> Source: Day 13 > Development; IAP 2026 Milestones

> **ALGORITHM 3 — Child with developmental delay / regression**
> 1. Identify delay vs plateau vs true loss of skills; screen for acute red flags and seizures.
> 2. Check hearing and vision, preterm correction, family/pregnancy history, and domain-specific exam.
> 3. Use validated developmental tools and neurological examination; refer early intervention now, not after diagnostic certainty.
> 4. If dysmorphic, multisystem or unexplained GDD: clinical genetics and phenotype-directed CMA / exome-genome strategy.
> 5. If regression, episodic decompensation or organomegaly: metabolic/neurodegenerative differential and targeted urgent tests.
> Source: Day 13 > Development; Day 15 > Genetics

## 5. ASD, ADHD, specific learning disorder and language/sleep overlays

| Entity | Core discriminator | First action / common trap |
|---|---|---|
| ASD | Social-communication deficits AND restricted/repetitive patterns; joint-attention/gesture deficits | M-CHAT-R/F is screening, not diagnosis; refer for comprehensive assessment. |
| Isolated language delay | Language affected, but reciprocal social interest/gestures relatively preserved | Audiology and speech-language assessment; assess receptive language. |
| ADHD | Developmentally inappropriate inattention and/or hyperactivity/impulsivity across ≥2 settings with impairment | Rule out sleep loss, hearing/vision, anxiety, learning disorder; single-parent rating is insufficient. |
| Specific learning disorder | Persistent domain-specific reading/writing/math difficulty despite appropriate opportunity/intervention | Assess schooling, language, vision/hearing and cognitive context; not equivalent to low IQ. |
| Sleep-disordered breathing | Snoring with witnessed apnea, gasping, daytime behavioral issues | Sleep assessment/OSA referral; do not automatically label ADHD. |
| Developmental regression | Loss of skills, not mere slow acquisition | Urgent neurology/metabolic evaluation; avoid watchful waiting. |

> **LATEST PROTOCOL UPDATE | ASD screening age is framework-specific**
> - IAP 2026 emphasizes proactive identification and M-CHAT-R/F screening in toddlers aged 16–30 months with clinical assessment.
> - AAP/CDC (2026 description) recommends general developmental screening at 9, 18, 30 months and ASD-specific screening at 18 and 24 months. A positive screen is not a diagnosis.
> Source: IAP Neurodevelopment 2026 / CDC 2026

## 7. Nutrition: WHAT → WHY → HOW → RECOGNIZE → WHAT NEXT

> **FEEDING CHAIN**
> - WHAT: inadequate energy, protein or micronutrients impair growth and neurodevelopment; repeated infection increases demand and worsens absorption.
> - WHY: milk-transfer problems, inappropriate complementary-food density, restricted diet, chronic disease or poverty can drive growth faltering.
> - HOW: observe a complete breastfeed; quantify age-appropriate meal frequency, texture, diversity, appetite and illness.
> - RECOGNIZE: poor weight trajectory, edema, MUAC/WHZ thresholds, anemia, rickets, xerophthalmia, neurologic B12 signs.
> - WHAT NEXT: urgent SAM triage when indicated; skilled lactation/nutrition support; Indian supplementation programme where applicable; evaluate red flags.
> Source: Day 14 > Feeding/Nutrition

| Feeding stage | Guideline anchor | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| Birth | Initiate breastfeeding within first hour if clinically feasible | Colostrum is not to be discarded. |
| 0–6 completed months | Exclusive breastfeeding (no routine water/other feeds) | Assess transfer and growth, not just reported feed frequency. |
| At 6 completed months | Start safe, thick, diverse complementary foods while continuing breastfeeding | Dilute watery gruels have low energy density. |
| 6–8 months | Typically 2–3 meals/day | Progress texture, not only volume. |
| 9–23 months | Typically 3–4 meals/day plus 1–2 snacks as needed | Responsive feeding; do not force or use screens. |
| Up to 2 y or beyond | Continue breastfeeding | Complementary food must meet rising iron/zinc/energy needs. |

> **LATEST PROTOCOL UPDATE | Complementary feeding 2023 / infant feeding 2026**
> - WHO 2023 guideline covers 6–23 months and emphasizes diverse nutrient-dense foods, responsive feeding and context-specific recommendations.
> - WHO infant and young child feeding fact sheet updated 4 August 2026 retains breastfeeding within 1 hour, exclusive 6 months, and complementary foods at 6 months with breastfeeding to ≥2 years.
> - IAP 2026 infant-nutrition chapter is the default Indian clinical framing.
> Source: WHO 2023 / WHO 4 Aug 2026 / IAP Nutrition 2026

## 8. MAM/SAM — classification, emergency sequence and disposition

| Age 6–59 mo | Definition (WHO / IAP 2026) | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| SAM | WHZ <−3 OR MUAC <11.5 cm OR bilateral nutritional pitting edema | Medical complication, poor appetite or unstable child → facility/NRC; otherwise community pathway per programme. |
| MAM | WHZ ≥−3 and <−2 OR MUAC ≥11.5 and <12.5 cm, no edema | Community nutrition support with medical evaluation and follow-up. |
| Stunting | HAZ <−2; severe <−3 | Chronic linear-growth indicator, not interchangeable with SAM. |
| Underweight | WAZ <−2 | Does not distinguish acute from chronic causes. |

> **ALGORITHM 4 — Acute malnutrition triage (6–59 months)**
> 1. Measure MUAC and WHZ; examine both feet for nutritional pitting edema.
> 2. If any SAM criterion present, label SAM even if other measures look normal.
> 3. Assess appetite and danger signs: hypoglycemia, hypothermia, shock, severe infection, dehydration, anemia, altered sensorium.
> 4. Complicated SAM or failed appetite test → urgent facility-based stabilization; uncomplicated with good appetite → supervised community care.
> 5. During inpatient stabilization: prevent/treat hypoglycemia and hypothermia, assess infection and dehydration cautiously, correct electrolytes, start appropriate starter feeding.
> 6. After clinical stabilization/appetite return/edema improves: transition to catch-up feeding (F-100 or approved RUTF protocol), then rehabilitation and close relapse surveillance.
> Source: Day 14 > SAM / IAP 2026 / WHO 2023 & 2026

> **SAM TREATMENT DISCRIMINATORS**
> - F-75 ≈75 kcal/100 mL: low-protein/low-sodium starter diet for stabilization; F-100 ≈100 kcal/100 mL: rehabilitation/catch-up phase, not initial feeding in unstable SAM.
> - Rehydration in SAM is cautious; dehydration signs can be unreliable and fluid overload is hazardous. Use current programme-specific rehydration pathway.
> - Iron is generally deferred during initial unstable stabilization and added during rehabilitation once appetite and infection status improve, per protocol.
> - Do not assume every SAM child requires admission; appetite/medical complications decide setting.
> - WHO implementation guidance published 17 September 2026 updates operational interpretation of WHO 2023 recommendations; national adoption and product rules remain country-specific.
> Source: Day 14 > SAM; IAP 2026; WHO 2026 Implementation

## 9. Vitamins/minerals: recognize pattern, test and act

| Deficiency | Classic recognition | Testing / treatment principle |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin A | Night blindness, Bitot spots, xerophthalmia, keratomalacia | Urgent ocular care + therapeutic vitamin A under age-specific protocol; do not confuse with prophylaxis. |
| Vitamin D / calcium | Widened wrists, rachitic rosary, bowing, hypocalcemic seizures; high ALP | 25-OH D, calcium, phosphate, PTH, ALP; treat calcium intake and vitamin D; distinguish phosphopenic rickets. |
| Vitamin B12 | Macrocytosis, hypotonia, regression, developmental delay; maternal vegan/restricted diet | B12, CBC, MMA/homocysteine where available; neurologic treatment should not await all tests. |
| Iron | Microcytosis, pallor, pica, poor intake; low ferritin if not inflamed | Elemental iron dosing depends on treatment vs prophylaxis; evaluate thalassemia if indices discordant. |
| Zinc | Poor growth, dermatitis, alopecia, diarrhea; acrodermatitis enteropathica | Replace zinc; NHM diarrhea programme uses age-specific 14-day course. |
| Iodine | Goiter, hypothyroidism, developmental impairment | Iodized salt; thyroid function and neonatal screening; avoid indiscriminate iodine dosing. |
| Vitamin C | Pseudoparalysis, bleeding gums, subperiosteal hemorrhage, scorbutic rosary | Diet history + radiograph; treat vitamin C. |
| Vitamin K | Bleeding tendency, prolonged PT/INR | Differentiate deficiency from hepatic disease; age-appropriate prophylaxis at birth. |

> **LATEST PROTOCOL UPDATE | Indian vitamin A prophylaxis (NHM updated 7 Aug 2026)**
> - Dose 1: 100,000 IU at 9 months (with measles-containing vaccination).
> - Doses 2–9: 200,000 IU at 16–18 months and every 6 months thereafter until 5 years (9 total doses).
> - This is prevention; xerophthalmia treatment uses a separate age-specific high-dose schedule.
> Source: NHM Vitamin A page updated 7 Aug 2026; NHM IMNCI 2023

> **LATEST PROTOCOL UPDATE | Zinc for childhood diarrhea (NHM STOP Diarrhoea 2025)**
> - Age <2 months: programme does not recommend routine zinc under this campaign.
> - 2 to <6 months: 10 mg elemental zinc daily for 14 days; ≥6 months to 5 years: 20 mg daily for 14 days.
> - Give ORS/appropriate rehydration as the core diarrhea treatment; zinc is an adjunct.
> Source: NHM STOP Diarrhoea Campaign Guidance 2025

> **IRON / VITAMIN D — PREVENTION IS NOT TREATMENT**
> - Anemia Mukt Bharat: children 6–59 months receive IFA syrup twice weekly (formulation/programme-specific); therapeutic iron for iron-deficiency anemia is a separate mg/kg/day elemental-iron prescription.
> - Common therapeutic oral elemental iron for pediatric IDA is about 3 mg/kg/day (some protocols 3–6 mg/kg/day); calculate elemental, not salt mass; review adherence, response and underlying cause.
> - Routine vitamin D infant supplementation often uses 400 IU/day in common pediatric guidance; nutritional rickets treatment uses substantially higher supervised regimens plus calcium and follow-up, not a single universal stoss dose.
> Source: NHM Anemia Mukt Bharat; WHO vitamin D/rickets guidance

## 10. Nutrition management calculations and data interpretation

| Formula | Worked example / caution |
|---|---|
| BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)^2 | 20 kg at 1.10 m → 20/1.21 = 16.53 kg/m²; interpret on age/sex chart. |
| WHZ, HAZ, WAZ | Use WHO LMS calculator/chart; (value − median)/SD is only a rough conceptual shortcut. |
| Elemental iron (mg/day) = mg/kg/day × weight | 3 mg/kg/day × 12 kg = 36 mg elemental/day; salt content differs by preparation. |
| Feed volume (mL) = target kcal / formula kcal per mL | F-75 0.75 kcal/mL: 600 kcal ≈800 mL; this is arithmetic, NOT a bedside SAM starting order. |
| Vitamin A volume = IU needed / IU per mL | 100,000 IU / 100,000 IU/mL = 1 mL; confirm actual bottle concentration. |
| Annualized height velocity | 4 cm in 8 months → 4 ×12/8 = 6 cm/year. |

## 11. Genetics and dysmorphology: mechanism → test → next step

> **GENETIC REASONING CHAIN**
> - WHAT: chromosome dosage, copy number, sequence variants, repeats, imprinting, mitochondrial inheritance and multifactorial traits create recognizable phenotypes.
> - WHY: the assay must match variant class; a normal karyotype cannot rule out a microdeletion or monogenic disorder.
> - HOW: three-generation pedigree + pregnancy/birth history + growth, HC, proportions, major/minor anomalies and targeted organ screening.
> - RECOGNIZE: aneuploid phenotype, syndrome-specific facial/cardiac pattern, hypotonia/hyperphagia, congenital anomalies, regression or biochemical decompensation.
> - WHAT NEXT: choose karyotype/CMA/methylation/repeat assay/exome-genome/biochemical testing; begin phenotype-directed surveillance and counselling.
> Source: Day 15 > Genetics / IAP Genetics 2026

| Test | Detects best | Major blind spot / pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| Karyotype | Aneuploidy, large rearrangements, balanced translocations | Small CNVs and single-nucleotide variants. |
| CMA / SNP array | Genome-wide deletions/duplications, some UPD/isodisomy patterns | Balanced rearrangements; small sequence variants; methylation status. |
| FISH / MLPA | Targeted known deletion/duplication | Not genome-wide in typical use. |
| Methylation 15q11-q13 | Imprinting signature of PWS/AS | Some Angelman UBE3A sequence variants have normal methylation. |
| Exome/genome | Coding / broader sequence variants; genome may capture more structural/intronic variants | A negative result does not exclude all repeat, methylation, mosaic or mtDNA defects. |
| Repeat-expansion test | FMR1, Huntington, myotonic dystrophy, etc. | Conventional exome may miss repeat expansions. |
| Biochemical metabolomics | Amino acids, acylcarnitines, urine organic acids, ammonia | Screening biomarkers are not definitive genotypes. |

> **LATEST PROTOCOL UPDATE | ACMG 2021 + IAP 2026 genomic-test selection**
> - ACMG 2021 strongly recommends considering exome/genome sequencing as first- or second-tier for congenital anomalies, developmental delay or intellectual disability.
> - IAP 2026 emphasizes phenotype-directed testing: CMA for suspected CNV; exome for heterogeneous monogenic conditions; targeted assay for known syndrome or repeat.
> - Do not use the outdated absolute rule “CMA must always precede exome”.
> Source: ACMG 2021 Genetics in Medicine; IAP 2026 Advanced Genomic Testing

> **DYSMORPHOLOGY CLASSIFICATION — DO NOT CONFUSE TERMS**
> - Major anomaly: structural abnormality with substantial medical/functional or cosmetic impact; minor anomaly: limited clinical impact but may be a syndromic clue when multiple are present.
> - Malformation: intrinsic abnormal morphogenesis (e.g. cleft lip); deformation: extrinsic mechanical molding (e.g. positional clubfoot); disruption: breakdown of previously normal tissue (e.g. amniotic band); dysplasia: disordered tissue organization (e.g. skeletal dysplasia).
> - Sequence: one initiating defect leads to downstream anomalies (Pierre Robin sequence: micrognathia → glossoptosis → cleft palate/airway issues).
> - Association: nonrandom co-occurrence without one established unifying etiology (VACTERL); syndrome: recognizable pattern with a common cause or mechanism.
> Source: Day 15 > Dysmorphology / IAP Evaluation of a Dysmorphic Child 2026

## 12. Syndromes and inheritance — close discriminator table

| Syndrome | Genetic mechanism | Clue and critical screening |
|---|---|---|
| Down | Trisomy 21, translocation or mosaicism | Hypotonia, single palmar crease, AVSD; thyroid/hearing/vision/OSA and hematology surveillance. |
| Turner | 45,X or mosaic / structural X | Short stature, webbed neck, left-sided cardiac/renal anomalies; karyotype; monitor aorta/hearing/thyroid. |
| Klinefelter | 47,XXY | Tall adolescent, small firm testes, hypergonadotropic hypogonadism, learning/language issues. |
| 22q11.2 deletion | Microdeletion | Conotruncal anomaly, hypocalcemia, palatal defect, T-cell deficiency; immune evaluation before live vaccines. |
| Williams | 7q11.23 deletion incl. ELN | Supravalvular aortic stenosis, hypercalcemia tendency, sociable phenotype; CMA. |
| Prader–Willi | Loss of paternal expression 15q11-q13 | Neonatal hypotonia/poor feeding → childhood hyperphagia; methylation testing. |
| Angelman | Loss of maternal neuronal UBE3A | Severe speech impairment, ataxia, seizures, frequent laughter; methylation/UBE3A testing. |
| Fragile X | FMR1 CGG repeat expansion | Long face, large ears, macroorchidism after puberty, ASD/GDD; targeted repeat assay. |
| Trisomy 18 | Extra chromosome 18 | Overlapping clenched fingers, rocker-bottom feet, severe growth restriction. |
| Trisomy 13 | Extra chromosome 13 | Holoprosencephaly, polydactyly, cleft, scalp defects. |

> **INHERITANCE / COUNSELLING FACTS**
> - Autosomal recessive carrier × carrier: each pregnancy 25% affected, 50% carrier, 25% unaffected noncarrier.
> - Autosomal dominant heterozygote × unaffected: 50% transmission if penetrance not considered.
> - X-linked recessive carrier mother: 50% of sons affected; 50% of daughters carriers (simplified, unaffected father).
> - Mitochondrial mtDNA variants are maternally transmitted; heteroplasmy creates variable severity.
> - PWS: loss of paternally expressed genes; Angelman: loss of maternal neuronal UBE3A; methylation detects most imprinting mechanisms.
> Source: Day 15 > Inheritance/Imprinting; NHS Genomics Knowledge Hub

## 13. IEM emergency: pattern → mechanism → action

| Biochemical phenotype | Think first | Diagnostic discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperammonemia + respiratory alkalosis, often no marked acidosis | Urea cycle defect | Plasma amino acids, citrulline, urine orotic acid. |
| High anion-gap metabolic acidosis + ketosis ± hyperammonemia | Organic acidemia (PA/MMA etc.) | Urine organic acids, acylcarnitine C3, B12 status. |
| Hypoketotic hypoglycemia + high FFA | Fatty-acid oxidation defect | Acylcarnitine pattern, e.g. C8 in MCAD. |
| Hypoketotic hypoglycemia + suppressed FFA | Hyperinsulinism | Critical sample and glucagon response; distinct from FAOD. |
| Lactic acidosis + multisystem / neurologic findings | Mitochondrial / pyruvate metabolism | Lactate-pyruvate context, MRI, gene testing; lactate can be normal intermittently. |
| Organomegaly + coarse facies + regression | Lysosomal storage disorder | Enzyme testing, urine GAGs / specific metabolites, genetics. |
| Hypotonia + leukodystrophy + VLCFA elevation | Peroxisomal disorder | VLCFA panel and targeted genetics. |

> **ALGORITHM 5 — Suspected IEM decompensation**
> 1. Stabilize ABC, shock and seizures; evaluate/treat sepsis in parallel.
> 2. Stop catabolic stress: halt offending protein/long-chain fat intake briefly according to suspected disorder; start adequate IV glucose and specialist-directed lipids.
> 3. Before treatment if immediately feasible: blood gas, glucose, electrolytes/anion gap, lactate, ammonia (proper sample handling), ketones, LFT, CBC, blood culture, plasma amino acids/acylcarnitines and urine organic acids.
> 4. Classify dominant pattern: hyperammonemia/respiratory alkalosis, ketotic acidosis, hypoketotic hypoglycemia or lactic/multisystem.
> 5. Consult metabolic ICU early; use nitrogen scavengers/arginine or other cofactors only per confirmed/suspected defect and local specialist protocol.
> 6. If severe or rising ammonia, arrange urgent extracorporeal clearance; do not wait for molecular diagnosis.
> 7. Reintroduce appropriate nutrition early after stabilization under metabolic supervision and plan family counselling.
> Source: Day 15 > Sick Neonate/IEM; CHOP pathway; PCRRT consensus

> **LATEST PROTOCOL UPDATE | Hyperammonemia escalation — do NOT blend thresholds**
> - CHOP institutional neonatal pathway: mild <200 µmol/L without encephalopathy; moderate 200–500; severe ≥500 or severe encephalopathy/respiratory alkalosis. It uses a high anti-catabolic GIR (10–15 mg/kg/min) in selected neonatal pathways.
> - PCRRT 2020 expert consensus: ammonia >500 µmol/L is a published dialysis indication; 301–499 with moderate/severe encephalopathy should trigger scavengers and dialysis preparation. Another 2022 consensus favors lower dialysis thresholds in young children; these are not identical rules.
> - Emergency action is based on neurologic status, ammonia trajectory and specialist protocol, not a single memorized threshold. Scavenger infusion doses require pharmacy/metabolic team verification.
> Source: CHOP Neonatal Hyperammonemia pathway; PCRRT 2020; 2022 consensus

| Urea cycle defect | Citrulline / other marker | Orotic acid / clue |
|---|---|---|
| NAGS or CPS1 | Very low citrulline | Normal/low orotic acid; NAGS may respond to carglumic acid. |
| OTC | Low citrulline | High urinary orotic acid (carbamoyl phosphate overflow). |
| ASS1 (citrullinemia I) | Markedly high citrulline | Argininosuccinate absent/low; can cause neonatal crisis. |
| ASL deficiency | Elevated citrulline + argininosuccinate | Argininosuccinic acid detectable; brittle hair/trichorrhexis nodosa. |
| ARG1 deficiency | Elevated arginine | Spasticity often later; arginine therapy is not routine in ARG1 deficiency. |

> **IEM MUST-RECOGNIZE TREATABLE CLUES**
> - PKU: high phenylalanine; dietary phenylalanine restriction with tyrosine adequacy; screen-positive must be confirmed.
> - Classic galactosemia: jaundice/hepatopathy, E. coli sepsis, cataracts after milk feeds; immediately remove galactose/lactose pending evaluation.
> - MSUD: maple-syrup odor, neonatal encephalopathy, elevated branched-chain amino acids / alloisoleucine; metabolic crisis treatment urgent.
> - Homocystinuria (CBS): downward lens dislocation + thrombosis + marfanoid habitus; pyridoxine responsiveness varies.
> - MCAD: fasting-induced hypoketotic hypoglycemia + C8 elevation; avoid fasting and give prompt dextrose during illness.
> - MMA vs PA: both may elevate C3; MMA and methylmalonate differ from PA; check B12/cobalamin defects and maternal B12 deficiency.
> - Pompe: infant cardiomyopathy/hypotonia; Gaucher: hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias; MPS: coarse facies, organomegaly, dysostosis.
> - X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy: VLCFA elevation; Zellweger spectrum: hypotonia, seizures, dysmorphism, VLCFA elevation.
> Source: Day 15 > IEM pattern recognition

## 14. Newborn screening: screening ≠ diagnosis

> **ALGORITHM 6 — Positive newborn screen**
> 1. Identify exact analyte, specimen age, gestation, transfusion/TPN context and programme cutoffs.
> 2. Contact family and metabolic/endocrine service promptly; assess whether child is symptomatic.
> 3. If unwell, stabilize and treat immediately; do not wait for confirmatory result.
> 4. Order condition-specific confirmatory plasma/urine enzyme or molecular testing.
> 5. Explain false positives, false negatives and programme-panel variability; arrange follow-up and family testing.
> Source: Day 15 > Newborn Screening

> **SCREENING LIMITATIONS**
> - India does not have a single universal expanded newborn screening panel covering every state and facility; local programme rules vary.
> - Tandem MS detects characteristic metabolites, not the causal genotype. Elevated C3 is not diagnostic of propionic acidemia alone.
> - A normal newborn screen does not rule out a metabolic disorder in a sick child; symptoms always override screening reassurance.
> - Congenital hypothyroidism screening and treatment are programme-/assay-specific; obtain confirmatory venous TSH and free T4 and treat promptly when indicated, without delaying for imaging.
> Source: Day 15 > Newborn Screening / ISPAE/Indian programme context

## 17. Thirty-five ultra-high-yield exam pearls

1. Choose the correct chart before interpreting any percentile. [D13 > Charts]
2. Height velocity and target height matter more than a single height point. [D13 > Growth]
3. WHO 0–5 y; IAP 2015 reference 5–18 y in India. [D13 > Charts]
4. Indian IAP BMI cutoffs use adult-equivalent 23 and 27 curves. [D13 > Charts]
5. Weight-first faltering points to nutrition/systemic illness; height-first with preserved weight suggests endocrine cause. [D13 > Patterns]
6. Familial short stature: normal velocity, BA ≈ chronological age, short family. [D13 > Short Stature]
7. Constitutional delay: delayed puberty and bone age, often familial late maturation. [D13 > Short Stature]
8. Disproportion demands skeletal/genetic assessment before labeling GH deficiency. [D13 > Short Stature]
9. Regression of any acquired developmental skill is an urgent red flag. [D13 > Development]
10. GDD involves at least two affected developmental domains in young children. [D13 > Development]
11. Preterm corrected age and PMA are not interchangeable. [D13 > Corrected Age]
12. ASD requires both social-communication and restricted/repetitive domains. [D13 > ASD]
13. M-CHAT-R/F is a screen; diagnosis is clinical/multidisciplinary. [D13 > ASD]
14. Poor gestures/joint attention favors ASD over isolated expressive delay. [D13 > ASD]
15. Sleep apnea can masquerade as inattention/behavioral difficulty. [D13 > Sleep]
16. Breastfeeding: first hour, exclusive six months, continue to two years or beyond. [D14 > BF]
17. Complementary food at six months must be thick, diverse and energy dense. [D14 > CF]
18. Responsive feeding is not coercion or screen distraction. [D14 > CF]
19. Any one SAM criterion is sufficient, including bilateral pitting edema. [D14 > SAM]
20. Complicated SAM or poor appetite → facility; uncomplicated with appetite → community care. [D14 > SAM]
21. F-75 stabilizes; F-100/RUTF rehabilitates. [D14 > SAM]
22. Dehydration assessment in SAM is unreliable; avoid reflexive rapid fluid loading. [D14 > SAM]
23. Vitamin A prophylaxis and xerophthalmia treatment use different schedules. [D14 > Vitamin A]
24. India vitamin A: 100,000 IU at 9 mo, then 200,000 IU 8 more doses. [D14 > NHM]
25. India diarrhea zinc: 10 mg at 2–<6 mo; 20 mg ≥6 mo for 14 days. [D14 > NHM]
26. Elemental iron dose is not equal to ferrous salt mass. [D14 > Iron]
27. Low ferritin suggests iron deficiency; inflammation can elevate ferritin. [D14 > Iron]
28. Genetic test must match variant class: balanced rearrangement ≠ CNV ≠ SNV. [D15 > Tests]
29. ACMG 2021 supports exome/genome first- or second-tier for CA/DD/ID. [D15 > Genomics]
30. PWS loses paternal expression; Angelman loses maternal neuronal UBE3A. [D15 > Imprinting]
31. Conotruncal defect + hypocalcemia + palatal anomaly → 22q11.2 deletion. [D15 > Syndromes]
32. Hyperammonemia + respiratory alkalosis suggests UCD; ketotic acidosis suggests organic acidemia. [D15 > IEM]
33. OTC: low citrulline + HIGH urine orotic acid; CPS1/NAGS: low/normal orotic acid. [D15 > UCD; Notebook D10 Q65]
34. Hypoketotic hypoglycemia with high FFA → FAOD; low FFA → hyperinsulinism. [D15 > IEM]
35. Newborn screening is not diagnosis; symptoms override a negative screen. [D15 > NBS]
